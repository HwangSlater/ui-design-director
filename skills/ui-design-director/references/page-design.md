# Designing every page

Use this after `SITE-PLAN.md` is approved. Each page type passes two decisions before any production code: a **layout** choice from options that differ in structure, then a **mockup** approval at full fidelity. Work core page types first, then secondary ones. Keep `SITE-PLAN.md` status columns current as each decision lands.

## 1. Layout options

For each page type, produce 2–3 numbered layout options that differ in **structure**, not styling: what leads, how content is ordered, how the primary action is reached, how the page reflows on a phone.

- Render them with the approved `DESIGN.md` tokens — palette, fonts, spacing, components — and a licence-checked sample photo in every photo slot (see `comparison-board.md` § Sample photos). Keep colour, type and photos identical across options so only the structure differs. Users judged greyscale boxes as unable to show the real mood, and asked for photos always.
- Use the real or planned content from `SITE-PLAN.md` at real length.
- Capture each option at one wide and one real phone width (`scripts/capture.mjs`) and show the options in the viewer from `comparison-board.md`.
- Under each option state in one line what it prioritises and what it gives up.
- Vary the section containers down the page. A stay site whose first screen matched Stayfolio turned into a notice board below it — rooms, stay details, directions and refunds all in the same rounded tinted box — and the owner said it looked made, while the reference mixes photos and text like a magazine with few boxes. No more than two sections in a row may share a container; keep photos appearing all the way down (one per section is a good default when the reference does so).
- Carry the atmosphere into work screens. Date pickers, checkout and account pages fall back to a generic booking-app look ("like a hospital or hair-salon booking app; nothing hanok about it"); keep at least a photo band of the thing being booked, the heading face and one signature element on every such screen.
- An owner's screen shows what the owner acts on, not the customer's view reused: a calendar of who arrives in which room that night, not nightly prices and "only the main house left", which the owner read backwards.
- Make the options differ on the first phone screen, or say in the option's name where they start to differ ("2 — rooms right under the photo"). Two options whose first screens were identical read as the same file sent twice.
- Crop photos of people and animals on the face. Give every photo a focus point (`object-position` from a per-photo value, with the upload form letting the owner tap the face once) and check each crop at every aspect ratio it appears in: a shelter's sample puppy showed only nose and mouth in three cards and the owner said "a child's photo must show the eyes". One subject appears once per screen — the same dog as hero, door card and list card read as filler.
- Say each heading once. A band titled 「가족을 기다리는 아이 52마리」 followed by a section titled 「가족을 기다리는 아이들」 was the first thing the owner pointed out; the second section takes a different job or no heading.
- When the audience phones before it clicks (older donors, local shops, clinics), put phone and messenger as large buttons at the foot of every page, or a labelled empty slot until the numbers arrive. Small footer text with no number was the shelter owner's complaint.
- Do not hide controls behind a sideways scroll for an older audience. A sticky chip row cut at 「중형」 hid 「대형」 and 「나이·성격」 and the owner said her donors would not know to swipe; wrap to two lines or leave a visible part of the next chip.
- Make each row of a fact table one answer from the upload form, so the "unknown → hidden" rule can work: 「다른 동물: 개 예 · 고양이 모름」 showed an unknown the owner had been promised would never appear; it became 「개와 지내요: 예」 alone.
- Judge the first screen, and check anything that sticks (bottom action bar, sticky filters), with a one-screen capture at a few scroll positions (`capture.mjs` job with `"scrollY"`). A full-length capture stretches the window to the page height, so `100svh` first screens come out taller than on a laptop (a bakery's today list looked whole in the long shot and was cut at 900px), and it paints the bar halfway down the page over the text, and the owner read it as the button covering the story.
- Every form that takes a name, phone or address carries a personal-data consent line with purpose and retention (「개인정보 수집·이용에 동의해요 — 견적 상담에만 쓰고 3개월 뒤 지워요」, period marked 견본 until the owner sets it); a renovation quote form went out without one and the owner pointed to every platform having it. Ask the owner, before drawing a quote or booking form, which answers change the price or the date — rooms counted (one bathroom or two doubles the cost), the size people actually know (분양 평수, with 「모르면 비워 두세요」), a move-in date to meet, whether they live there during the work — and ask those, not a generic contact form.
- An empty optional block disappears entirely; do not replace it with a sentence explaining that it is empty — 「이 사례엔 손님 후기가 아직 없어요」 reads to customers as 「후기 없는 집」.
- Fix the terms before the second page type, not at the end. Once a flow spans several pages, run `/ui-wording` on everything drawn so far and record the term table in `DESIGN.md`: one shelter mockup said 「매달 후원 / 한 번 후원」 on the form, 「정기후원」 in the link under it and 「정기후원 / 일시후원」 in the earlier preview, and the owner asked for the one her donors know from banks.
- Sample states must be possible under the rules printed on the same screen. A volunteer date marked 「비로 취소」 five days ahead sat under "cancelled by text the day before"; set the sample "today" so every state can be true.
- A choice that ends the flow removes the way forward. An under-age answer that showed the refusal and the alternative still kept a 「다음」 button, and the owner asked where it would go.
- On every screen, customer or owner, a selected option looks the same for every option (filled the same way), and only things that can be pressed look pressable: status words are not buttons, progress marks are filled circles rather than empty boxes a customer will try to tick. Two clients in a row (a shelter, a bakery) raised this. On owner screens, a change that the public sees at once gets an undo. A shelter's status buttons moved a dog to the public "adopted" list on one gloved mis-tap, and "상담 중" kept the public outline style when chosen; the owner asked for undo on that one change and a filled selection everywhere.
- Personal data appears only after the identity check that the page itself mentions. A donor page showed bank digits and amount above "we verify by text code".
- Prototype interactions with a timer (an undo toast) are also built as their own page; the capture tool settles for several seconds before shooting and a short toast is gone by then.
- When the chosen home page leads with the same list as a section page, give the home page its own first line (the organisation's name and one sentence). Otherwise the menu item seems to do nothing: a shelter home and its 「입양」 page shared title, chips and the first four cards.
- A sample photo appears in one place across the main flow, not only once per screen. The same forest-walk photo as the home volunteer card, the donate page's "other ways" card and the about-page hero made the owner ask whether that was the only photo.
- Small organisations are trusted for the people behind them. Leave labelled slots for a staff or volunteer photo and a short "this month" news item when the reference shows activity news, rescue stories or people; a shelter mockup with none of these read thinner than the local NGOs beside it.
- Try each photo slot with a dim phone photo as well as the polished sample. Commons photos make any layout look good; the owner warned that her manager's phone shots would change the look. A sample photo shows only things the business makes: a bakery owner rejected a shelf of cherry danishes and twisted sticks the shop never bakes. When no free photo fits the request (a wooden display shelf), use one that is true to the menu (baguettes on a cooling rack) and ask the owner for that one photo.
- At least one option changes the **first screen** itself, not only the order of sections below it: full-width photo, type only, the timetable or price list first, a single strong object drawing. Three options that all open with text on the left and a photo on the right are one option.

**Composition first (desktop).** Before anything else, capture the reference home at 1280 and list its sections top to bottom with what fills each one (full-bleed photo, colour panel overlapping it, icon tiles, card grid, colour band with big numbers, editorial story grid, people/quote band, photo + panel call to action, dark footer). Build our page in that order at 1280 first, at the reference's proportions, with our content; only then fold it to the phone. Give the identity its own assets: a display face for headlines (a heavy Korean display face such as Black Han Sans at 56–72px when the reference uses big condensed type), two or three strong block colours with roles, and icons drawn in the site's own style (filled circle in a brand colour, heavy stroke), not a thin generic set. Run `node scripts/compose_metrics.mjs <out.md> --width 1280 --ours ours ours=<file url> ref1=<url> ref2=<url> …` and rework until no line reads 「미달」; record the table next to the mockup. List and menu pages get the same check against the reference sites' page of the same type (a bakery menu page against Flour's menu and 태극당's product list): three text-first options measured 36/16/2% photo on the first screen against 55–78%, and the home page's photo-plus-dark-panel header fixed all three. Re-measure after adding any band or strip — a bake-time strip above the list pushed the first photo down to 45% and failed. Then build a blind sheet with `python scripts/blind_sheet.py <dir> 우리=<ours.jpg> ref=<ref.jpg> …` (same crop, logos greyed, shuffled, answer key kept apart) and give its `prompt.txt` to a fresh agent; above 70% "AI-made" on ours sends it back while the judge still names fixable tells (see "Reading the blind judge" below — content-level tells set a floor no layout change moves). Hide 「견본」 marks in the judged screen (a `clean` body class) — on a screen they read as unfinished. Fill the judged screen as the finished site will be: a photo-grid option with 12 of 15 hatched slots loses to a list for no reason of layout, so give each slot its own sample photo there.

What the blind judge picked out as the generated formula, so do not build it: a stock photo with a floating colour box holding a bold headline, two buttons (filled + outline) and four icon cards overlapping the photo's bottom edge; the display face used for every name and card title (it should carry one or two levels only); thin generic line icons; photos with mismatched light and colour; no brand asset of its own. What moved it toward the running sites: colour panel and photo split side by side instead of a box floating on the photo, a name tag inside the photo that tells one animal's story, a working quick finder (Dogs Trust's "Find dogs to adopt") instead of icon cards, one colour grade across all photos, one repeated shape of the site's own (a petal corner on photos, a brush-stroke edge between bands), and double the section spacing.

Reading the blind judge. Put one real site of the same field in a sheet of its own each time you change the controls (`우리=<real site>` with its name masked): if it comes back above 30% the controls or the masks are wrong, not your page. Give every image its own logo box (`이름=<path>@x,y,w,h`): a fixed top-left box once covered a centred-logo mockup's notice bar and three judges called the "cut-off bar" an AI tell. Size it to the logo only: the default 420px box also hid a tea farm's menu next to its logo, and the judge's first tell was "no menu, no cart". What the judge reads as real is operating residue — photos the shop took (its own sign, street signs, price cards), banners the owner made, platform parts (Cafe24 login rows, Kakao chat button), leftover template text such as a button still reading 「BUTTON」 — and what it reads as AI is a page that is complete and consistent: live status shown as app UI (dots, badges), every line in one warm voice (even the owner's real sentences were called "AI acting the owner"), a key-value rules table, warm brown and one orange button. Only the first group can be bought with real material, so ask for the owner's photos at the brief (storefront with sign, counter, hands at work, the day's shelf, interior) and say early that sample photos cap the score. Do not imitate mess to win the judge; fix what is wrong, then stop and report.

The second blind round named the next layer of tells — the "nicely made mockup kit": a tilted name tag, one oversized rounded corner on every photo, pill buttons and chips everywhere, two colours with one headline line in the accent. Use one motif that comes from the name or field instead (a wildflower for a shelter called 들꽃) and carry it through logo, heading marks, list bullets and dividers. And fill the page the way a running organisation does: a utility bar (hours, phone, messenger), five quick links with one filled icon style, a dated news list, an Instagram strip, a footer with several columns — labelled 「견본」 for the client, hidden for the judge. Say a count once.

Small Korean shops rarely run a homepage — a search for neighbourhood bakery sites found only Naver Place, Daangn and Instagram profiles — so for them the running-site reference for operational traces (address and map, hours, notice line, Instagram, business info) is the Naver Place listing and the Instagram profile. Calibrate the judge and judge fairly. A control sheet of running sites only (two famous, three small regional rescues) came back all "real" at 80–92%, so the judge does not simply call the unfamiliar one AI. Still, mix our screen with lesser-known running sites of the same field rather than only famous ones, run two judges on different shuffles and average, and treat one run as noise (the same mockup moved between 5 and 6.5 across runs). Put exactly one of our screens in a sheet (two variants of the same client share a name and photos and give each other away), use controls from the same country and language (a lone Korean site among English ones stands out by language alone), and leave out the site you traced (the judge recognised the borrowed emblem, curve and vertical line). In a fair Korean round the information-dense direction (utility bar, dated news, operational details on each card) scored with or above running Korean shelters; the direction that borrowed one site's signature devices scored lowest. What still read as generated: every card filled to the same template, one decorative mark repeated before every heading and arrow after every link, the accent colour on every highlight, and features a small local organisation would not have (a filter bar for 50 animals). Placeholders end the verdict on their own — 「031-000-0000」 and an invented street name were the first tell named; in the judged screen hide invented contacts and addresses instead of showing dummies.

Before drawing options, open the reference's page that does the same job (and one or two other well-known sites in the field) and measure it: `node scripts/extract_styles.mjs <url> <out.json> --width 390`, and again at 1280. Copy the numbers that carry its look into the tokens — type scale, corner radius, shadow or none, button and input size, section spacing, how many photos per screen against how many boxes — and record them in `DESIGN.md` § Tokens with the reference as the source. Lay the page out section by section against the reference's sequence, keeping its screenshots open while you build, rather than composing from the written rules. Take components from a known design system or library, or the reference's own pattern, instead of drawing new ones: name the source for the calendar, sheet, gallery, form and tabs. Borrow a pattern whole — list its parts when you find it and check them off when you build it: the donation pattern found in research was three amounts plus "other amount", the mockup shipped without the free-amount field, and the owner said many donors give 5,000 or 20,000 a month.

Carry the visual layers recorded in `DESIGN.md` into every option — identity, item faces, icons, colour blocks, data marks, wide-screen columns — so options differ in structure while each looks as finished as the reference. A page of text on grey with white cards is not a layout option, it is a wireframe in colour. Then write down what makes the chosen reference good on this page type, from captures viewed at 1:1: image size relative to the screen, white space between sections, type hierarchy (how many sizes, how much contrast between them), density, and its signature components. Every option keeps those traits; options differ in structure, not in quality.

Derive options from the page's job and content. Do not pick a fashionable shape and fill it. When hallmark is installed, its named landing-page shapes can label read-surface options so they are clearly distinct (`companion-skills.md`); name the shape after deriving it, not before. Useful questions per surface:

- **Read surface:** What must the visitor believe before acting, and what proves it? Does the proof lead (product capture, figure, quote) or does the claim lead? Where does the primary action repeat? How long can the page be before the action is lost?
- **Work surface:** What does the user look at most often, and what do they change most often? Is the layout list → detail, master–detail split, canvas with panels, or stepwise? What stays fixed when content scrolls? What happens at 0, 1, and 1,000 items?

Set times and prices in the body face with tabular figures; a monospace face for `14:00 ~ 19:00` read as generated to the user. Recommend one option with the reason tied to the job, then let the user choose by number, combine by named part ("2's header with 1's list"), or reject. Mark the page type's `Layout` column `approved` only on the user's choice or explicit delegation.

When many page types share a frame (navigation, footer, sidebar, page header), settle that shared frame first on the most important page type and reuse it; do not re-offer it on every page.

## 2. Mockups (full fidelity)

Once a layout is approved, build the page as a full-fidelity mockup using only `DESIGN.md` tokens and components.

### Where mockups live

Keep mockups outside production routes, in one directory the user can open directly (default `mockups/` at the project root unless the user names another place), with an `index.html` that links every page and state. Build them as static HTML/CSS that load one shared `tokens.css` and one `components.css`; pages contain layout only. This makes the later code step a transfer of approved parts rather than a rewrite, and lets one token change restyle every mockup.

If the project already has a component library or framework that runs locally, build mockups with it on a non-production route instead, so the approved mockup is already close to code.

### What a mockup must show

- every section or region at real content length, including the longest realistic title, name, and label;
- each piece of information once per screen: a hero visual that repeats the table below it ("why does the timetable appear twice?") is decoration pretending to be content;
- one consistent moment in time. When a sample screen is set on a day ("today = 27 October"), every number on it must be true as of that day: a pre-match card next to standings taken after that match showed eight games played when only seven had been, and the client caught it. Pick the day the supplied data describes (the day after the round it covers) and fill only what lies after it as samples;
- sample data that agrees with itself, computed from one data file the build script reads. Write times, stock states, prices and counts once (`site.json`) and let every screen derive its line from it; retyped copies drift — a bakery home said 「소금빵은 하루 네 번」 while the bake table listed it once, and the list said 「바게트 있어요」 at 10:40 under 「바게트 1시 나올 예정」. Shared lines (「다 팔렸어요 · 오후 1시에 또 나와요」) are one component used by every page. Weekdays are computed from dates. Totals must add up (a 4 : 1 score lists four goals), the same team or roster has the same members on every page, and one snapshot date governs every count (places left, confirmed, waiting). One item's gallery shows only that item: a shelter preview filled one dog's thumbnails with two other dogs from the list and the owner asked "who is this?" — when there is only one sample photo, leave labelled slots for the rest. Never give a real name the user supplied a role they did not state: the client's top scorers turned into invented team captains read as fact. Write counts with their unit (「이준석 2골」, not 「이준석 2」) or readers count names. A fresh reviewer given only the screenshots and the plan caught most of these;
- tab titles from the page's own heading (「보리 · 들꽃쉼터」), never the layout option's label: 「2 아이들 먼저」 would show in the browser tab and in shared links;
- only facts the user gave. Anything you had to fill in — directions, walking times, opening days, policies, prices, staff names — is marked on the page as unconfirmed (for example a small "확인 필요" tag) and listed when you hand the mockup over. Shop rules and product descriptions are never filled in, not even with a 견본 mark — leave an empty slot for the owner's words: a bakery mockup told customers 「영어·이모티콘은 안 돼요」 (the shop pipes HAPPY BIRTHDAY and hearts) and 「딸기 철이 아닐 땐 냉동 딸기」, a line the owner said would stop anyone buying. Plausible guesses read as facts: a client found an invented subway exit, walking time and closing day only because they happened to read every line. FAQ answers and step durations are facts too — 「보통 2주쯤」, 「10분쯤」 and 「3분 안에」 were written without a source and the owner caught the first;
- for food, the major allergens on every item, present or absent (「호두 들어가요 · 우유·계란 없어요」 — naming only walnut made the owner ask about the rest); butter counts as milk (「우유(버터 포함)」), and an ingredient in every item (wheat in bread) is one line at the top;
- the page at narrow (≈375px), medium (≈768px), and wide (≈1280px+) widths;
- for work surfaces, each required state from `SITE-PLAN.md` as its own labelled frame: empty, loading, error, no-permission, dense data. A pay or submit button that can be pressed with fields missing shows the case: a banner names what is missing with links, the page moves to the first missing field, and each field carries its own message; text over a length limit stays in the field with 「2자 넘었어요」 so the customer chooses what to cut; a slot taken while choosing says so and keeps everything else; a failed payment says no money left the account, in words that fit every payment method. When the customer needs to know the shop saw the order, show a progress list (paid → sent to the shop → the shop confirmed) whose last line changes only when staff press confirm;
- interactive components in default, hover, keyboard focus, active, disabled, and error/invalid states where they appear;
- only the motion `DESIGN.md` approved, built and checked against emilkowalski/skills when installed (`companion-skills.md`);
- real imagery when supplied; otherwise licence-checked sample photos of the right subject and aspect ratio, credited and labelled on the page as samples to be replaced, with a hatched frame as the offline fallback. Do not use generated imagery, and do not present sample photos as the product.
- the same day on every state of a screen (see the bullet on one moment in time): pick its date and derive every state from it: a tea farm's palette preview carried a 「설 선물 마감」 band (winter) above a first-flush tea on sale (sold out every May) and spring booking dates open (booking starts 1 March), and the owner caught all three.
- unavailable things — sold out, full, not yet open — as greyed text plus the next thing the visitor can do (「알림 받기」), never struck through — two owners said a line through the words makes them unreadable.
- Descriptions the client did not give (what a tea tastes like, how it is made) carry 「확인 필요」 until the client writes them, even when they are true.
- Sample data is one data set, not per-screen text. Keep seats, orders and sample customers in one file that both the customer screens and the owner screens read, and derive every count from it (seats left = capacity − booked − held; 「오늘 보낼 것」 = orders since yesterday's cut-off): a tea farm's owner screen said 「오늘 보낼 것 2」 with three cards listed, sent a rain notice to 「9명」 on a day with 7 web bookings plus a 10-person phone group, and showed the customer's 5월 8일 family under 5월 1일. When a state changes the page, everything that depends on it changes with it: an 「11명이면 단체라 전화로」 notice sat above a live 「4명 예약하고 결제하기」 button and total.
- Sample data obeys its own controls: the chips drawn as selected match every item in the list below them (「일산 · 욕실」 selected above a 김포 kitchen was caught), 「전체」 contains every item including the one featured at the top, and one sample case keeps the same photos and numbers on every screen and option.
- Product lists sell with photos. Once product photos exist, use the reference shop's card grid at its thumbnail size (two columns on a phone, three or four at 1280); a text row with a 64px thumbnail and a 「담기」 button read as an order form to a blind judge even with the owner's real prices. Until the photos arrive, say plainly that the list will grow into cards and that the inner pages cap the score: a tea farm's first screen scored 8 with the owner's field photo while the product pages, all slots, scored 5.5.
- before/after and featured items laid out without the tells judges named: two blind judges of a renovation home (stand-in before/after photos) named the same layout tells: captions under paired photos collided with the line below (put 「고치기 전/고친 뒤」 as a tag on the photo's corner), the case featured at the top repeated as the first card right under it (on the home page start the list from the next one and link to the full list), a two-column caption with one small line on the left left a hole, and cards with hatched slots next to filled ones (once photos arrive, list only items that have them — the upload form requires photos anyway).
- Korean labels that never break mid-word on a phone (「체 험」, 「장바구 니」 when a cart link was added): `white-space: nowrap` on nav items and short buttons, and drop the least-used item from the phone header rather than letting it wrap.
- Capture owner and staff options at 390 before sending them too: two inputs side by side in a grid overflowed the phone (inputs keep a default width; give grid children `min-width: 0` and inputs `width: 100%`), and the owner could not see the end of 「600만 원대」.

### Self-check before showing

First hold every name, number and date on the page against the brief: list each one as **given** (the user said it), **derived** (computed from what they gave — a weekday, a deadline three days later, standings maths) or **invented**. Invented items that a reader could take as real — people's names, scores, prices, dates, counts — get a 「견본」 mark; filler that cannot be mistaken (an empty-state line, example text inside an empty input) does not. Never put a mark on top of a photo or over the first screen — on an image it reads as part of the copy; write one plain line under it instead ("사진은 모두 견본이에요"). Mark at the smallest unit that carries the risk and at most once per block: a client stopped reading marks that appeared four times a screen, and still had to catch an invented player name, a captain's role given to a real scorer, a score whose scorers did not add up and a word left from another project — each one a check against this list would have caught.

When re-cutting a chosen option into the mockup, diff its text against the option the user chose and report every line that disappeared; a bakery client found 「소금빵은 하루 네 번 구워요」, a line she had asked for, silently dropped. Capture again right before every send and send only those paths — an old capture still showed a corrected date. Run `scripts/check_site.py` on the mockup directory and fix every `FLOOR` finding. Run the companion checks agreed at the start (impeccable detector, Web Interface Guidelines) and fix what they confirm. Then build the side-by-side page (`python scripts/side_by_side.py <out.html> <ours.jpg> <reference.jpg> …`) for the first screen and the densest page at phone and desktop width, fill in its layer table, and fix every layer the reference has and ours lacks without a recorded reason. Then name at least three places where the mockup is flatter, denser, more generic or less finished than the reference; fix them before showing. Anything you notice in your own screenshots is fixed before the user sees it, however small — a label that wraps one word onto its own line was spotted in review, sent anyway, and was the first thing the user pointed out. Then render the pages and check the places mockups break: overflow at the narrow width, the longest label, sticky or scrolling regions, and the states you claimed. Screenshot each page at the three widths. If no renderer is available, say the check was structural only.

Hand the mockups over as a **clickable prototype**, not as screenshots: link the pages so the main flows can be clicked through (logo, navigation, card → detail → add to cart → cart → checkout → order complete, account links), and give every page a small collapsed "상태" switcher on the edge of the screen that jumps between that page's states (empty, invalid, failure, notice/confirm, busy). Keep the switcher clear of toasts, fixed bars, dialogs and content at every width you hand over. For a read-only site handed to an owner who is not a developer (a local shop or school), leave the switcher out: describe the fallback states in one plain sentence ("인터넷이 느리면 사진 대신 빈칸이 보여요") and keep only prototype notices that answer a real question, such as what a not-yet-connected button will do; an owner found a 「시안 상태」 tab meaningless and it covered a caption at tablet width. Put prototype notes (links to other states) at the very bottom of the page, after the footer, headed so nobody mistakes them for content ("시안 안내 — 손님에게는 안 보여요"); placed under the introduction, a client took them for something guests would see. Prototype-only script and styles stay in separate files that never move into production code. Click the flows through in a real browser at phone and desktop width before handing over, then give the full path of the entry page and a short list of what to look at. Users found a screenshot viewer tedious once there were many pages and states. Use the screenshot viewer (`comparison-board.md`) for choosing between layout options, where side-by-side comparison matters. Ask for approval per page type, or for specific changes. Classify feedback with `design-md-format.md` § Feedback protocol: a change that should apply everywhere goes into `DESIGN.md` and `tokens.css`/`components.css`, not into one page.

## 3. Consistency across pages

After the core page types are approved, render them side by side and check that they read as one site: same navigation and footer, same heading scale for the same level, same spacing rhythm, same button hierarchy, same treatment of empty and error states. Fix drift at the shared layer.

A new component that appears on only one page is allowed; record it in `DESIGN.md` § Components when it is approved so the next page reuses it instead of inventing a variant.

## 4. Mockup approval gate

The site's mockups are approved when every core page type has `Mockup: approved` in `SITE-PLAN.md`. Secondary page types may stay pending if the user wants to start code; list them as remaining work. Do not begin production code for a page whose mockup is not approved unless the user explicitly asks.
