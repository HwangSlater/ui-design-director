## 2. Rules by topic

### 2.1 Process and habits
1. Lay out the page in greyscale first and add colour last, on purpose — the spacing, size and order problems surface before colour can hide them — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
2. Copy great work only in private studies, and publish only original work — public near-copies cost you opportunities, and studies that sit too close to their source are unethical to show — [Hische](https://www.jessicahische.is/thinkingthoughtsoninspiration)
3. Draw from many references, including historical ones, never just one or two — a single source shows through as a blend of that one designer — [Hische](https://www.jessicahische.is/thinkingthoughtsoninspiration)
4. Before showing a design, ask "does this look like someone I know?" and move away from the influence if it does — derivative work is recognisable — [Hische](https://www.jessicahische.is/thinkingthoughtsoninspiration)
5. Look for ideas outside the client's sector — the competitors in one sector copy each other, and better patterns often live elsewhere — [Boag](https://boagworld.com/emails/copy-competitors/)
6. Treat competitor sites as inputs, never as instructions. Watch for the argument "everyone in our sector does X" — competitors have often not researched their choices, and copied features may underperform for them too — [Boag](https://boagworld.com/emails/copy-competitors/)
7. Imitate the best work until you are good, and let a personal style emerge later — pursuing a style directly before you have the fundamentals produces weak work — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html), [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
8. When something you admire works, explain why in words, not just good or bad — taste is a trained pattern-reading skill and can be transferred to others, including agents — [Kowalski, developing taste](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste), [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
9. To teach an agent taste, write one strict rule file per aspect (motion, type, layout) with exact values and decision trees. Then have it review its own output against those rules and list issues with before/after — without exact rules it guesses or invents standards — [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
10. Review motion, radii and other fine details again the next day with fresh eyes — first-day judgement is unreliable — [Kowalski, great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations), [Malewicz, radius](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/your-radius-nesting-guide)
11. Write design principles that say what you will not do, in the form "we want X because Y", and test them against real screens — vague, visionary principles cannot be applied and lead to ad-hoc, dull work — [Friedman](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/04/practical-guide-design-principles/)
12. Start from an analogy ("like a Porsche 911"), pull out its qualities, then drop the analogy and keep the rules — this turns a mood into checkable criteria — [Friedman](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/04/practical-guide-design-principles/)
13. Question whether a section or feature should exist at all before polishing it, and show the core idea to real users early — late discovery is expensive — [Zhuo / Soleio](https://lg.substack.com/p/how-to-spot-a-world-class-designer)
14. Pick a direction and own it instead of averaging everyone's feedback — consensus designs come out bland — [Zhuo / Soleio](https://lg.substack.com/p/how-to-spot-a-world-class-designer)
15. In a critique, look past the visuals to copy, navigation, hierarchy and accessibility — "how could it work better" finds more than "how could it look better" — [Lovin, critique](https://brianlovin.com/writing/design-critique-for-fun-and-profit-7wDWkhw)
16. List the real requirements first, then shape the design to what plain HTML and CSS do well. Add no tooling or systems for complexity you do not have — small sites drown in the overhead — [Chimero, Everything Easy](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2018/everything-easy/)
17. Choose a typeface only after you understand the job, from a shortlist of suitable candidates — a default font chosen by elimination is a "thoughtless default" — [Santa Maria](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/the-sweatpants-of-typefaces)

### 2.2 Layout and space
18. Treat whitespace as the starting material and add elements into it, roughly doubling what feels enough. Menu items get about 2× the text height, and list groups get about 25px between them — unstyled defaults are cramped, and space creates hierarchy — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
19. Use three spacing tiers: between lines, between elements, and larger between groups — the size of the gap is what tells a viewer what belongs together — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html), [Malewicz, layout](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/my-secret-to-perfect-layout-revealed)
20. A working marketing-page grid is 12 columns of 64px with 32px gutters inside a 1120px container (above a 1280px viewport). Inside blocks, use 16px to group and 32px to separate. On mobile, use 32px outer margins, one column, and a 24px gap if two columns are needed — 12 divides into 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 columns — [Malewicz, layout](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/my-secret-to-perfect-layout-revealed)
21. Let content set its width and align elements to each other, rather than forcing everything into fixed columns. On mobile, three guides (16px left, centre, 16px right) are enough — rigid grids break on resizing screens — [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
22. Build the page up from its content and then size the container. Do not draw a fixed frame and cram content into it — the web has no fixed edges — [Chimero, Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)
23. Default to full-width vertical bands that stack, and plan for the text-beside-image problem: as the window widens, a fixed-ratio image grows taller while the text column shrinks — breakpoints only patch this, so design for it — [Chimero, Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)
24. Use fluid percentages for widths (target ÷ context). At each breakpoint, change the column count and re-check type size and tap targets — layouts must survive unknown viewports — [Marcotte](https://alistapart.com/article/responsive-web-design/)
25. Break up long pages with recurring "interludes": oversized type, a full photo, a colour field or a pull quote, all sharing one visual system — rhythm keeps people reading, and dumping text into one column does not — [Santa Maria, Interludes](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/interludes-in-design)
26. Align every element to something (a grid line, baseline, edge or optical centre), and nudge by ±1px where the eye disagrees with the geometry — perceived alignment matters more than numeric alignment — [Web Interface Guidelines (Freiberg)](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
27. Check every layout at mobile, laptop and ultra-wide (zoom to 50% to fake ultra-wide), and with short, average and very long content — real content breaks the ideal mockup — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
28. Make nested corner radii concentric: inner radius = outer radius − padding. If that gives zero or less, use a small 1–4px inner radius or raise the outer radius — mismatched radii look amateur — [Malewicz, radius](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/your-radius-nesting-guide), [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)

### 2.3 Hierarchy and labels
29. Rank the elements by importance and make visibility follow that order, using size, colour, contrast and weight — attention is the job — [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
30. Only the page title should be fully emphasised. Every other element should mix "up" levers (bigger, bolder, caps) with "down" levers (lighter colour, smaller, less margin), e.g. a big number in a light weight, or a small label in bold caps with tracking — maxing out every lever flattens hierarchy — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
31. Drop labels when the format already says what the data is (phone, email, price). Otherwise fold the label into the value ("12 left in stock", "3 bedrooms") — a "label: value" layout gives everything the same weight — [Schoger & Wathan](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/labels-are-a-last-resort)
32. When labels must stay, make them quieter than the value. Reverse this only on spec sheets where people scan for the label — emphasis should follow what the reader is looking for — [Schoger & Wathan](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/labels-are-a-last-resort)
33. Keep underline for links, and avoid text-background highlights and strikethrough for emphasis — they read as links, hover states or deletions — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
34. Hover and selected states should change colour, background, shadow or underline, never size, weight or case — those reflow the text and make the layout jump — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
35. Interactive states (hover, active, focus) must contrast more than the resting state — feedback has to be visible — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)

### 2.4 Typography
36. Body text on desktop: 18–24px for reading pages and 14–20px for interaction-heavy pages. On mobile: 16–20px (start at about 17px). Inputs are at least 16px so iOS does not zoom — readable defaults beat tiny "elegant" text — [Kennedy, font sizes](https://www.learnui.design/blog/mobile-desktop-website-font-size-guidelines.html)
37. Page titles: 35–50px on desktop and 28–40px on mobile, in a bold or heavier weight. Headings are the best place for a second font — [Kennedy, font sizes](https://www.learnui.design/blog/mobile-desktop-website-font-size-guidelines.html)
38. Use about 4 sizes in total (header, default, secondary, optional caption). Secondary text is about 2px smaller and about 70% strength in colour. Body, buttons, menus and inputs share the default size — fewer sizes look intentional — [Kennedy, font sizes](https://www.learnui.design/blog/mobile-desktop-website-font-size-guidelines.html)
39. Size type by how distinct each level needs to be, not by a golden-ratio scale. Make bigger jumps at large sizes and smaller jumps at small sizes — mathematical scales do not fix weak hierarchy — [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
40. Line length: 50–75 characters (about 9–12 words, about 65ch). Below about 30 characters per line on mobile, reduce the size — comprehension drops outside that band — [Kennedy, font sizes](https://www.learnui.design/blog/mobile-desktop-website-font-size-guidelines.html), [Malewicz, layout](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/my-secret-to-perfect-layout-revealed), [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
41. Tighten tracking as size grows: positive at small sizes, about −1% to −2% at display sizes. Inter's formula is tracking = −0.0223 + 0.185·e^(−0.1745·size) — big type set at default spacing looks loose and cheap — [Andersson](https://d.rsms.me/inter-website/v3/dynmetrics/), [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
42. Loosen the letter-spacing on small uppercase labels — caps set tight are hard to read — [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste), [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
43. Use tabular figures in prices and comparison columns. Use curly quotes, the single "…" character, no widows, and non-breaking spaces between numbers and units — these are cheap markers of care — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines), [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste), [Santa Maria, smart quotes (title)](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/)
44. Pick fallback fonts with matching metrics, and preload and subset the main font — this avoids layout shift while fonts load — [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste), [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
45. Beginners should prefer clean, neutral, well-made fonts (e.g. Satoshi, Metropolis, Source Sans, Figtree) over decorative ones, avoid overused picks like Montserrat, and avoid the thinnest weights in UI — decorative fonts are hard to control, and light weights fail on screens — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
46. A typeface can be part of a motif: letterforms that echo the logo shape (e.g. hexagonal forms for a hexagon brand) — this ties the type to the brand — [Kennedy, motif](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs-create-cohesive-brand.html)
47. Use a font change, a width change (condensed or extended) or a thick brand-coloured underline to highlight a phrase in a headline — this adds character without new decoration — [Kennedy, 37 ways](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs.html)

### 2.5 Colour
48. Build the palette from one base colour and derive its variants. Do not use named colour-wheel harmonies (complementary, split-complementary) — those schemes do not predict good UI — [Kennedy, colour framework](https://www.learnui.design/blog/color-in-ui-design-a-practical-framework.html), [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
49. For a darker shade, lower brightness and raise saturation together. For a lighter shade, raise brightness and lower saturation. Do not just add black or white — darkening by brightness alone looks muddy (right about 95% of the time) — [Kennedy, HSB](https://www.learnui.design/blog/the-hsb-color-system-practicioners-primer.html), [Kennedy, colour framework](https://www.learnui.design/blog/color-in-ui-design-a-practical-framework.html)
50. Shift hue slightly with lightness: darker shades toward red, green or blue (0/120/240°), lighter shades toward yellow, cyan or magenta (60/180/300°). Move a default blue from 240° to 210° for a casual feel, or to 260° for indigo. Nudge an error red toward pink to make it friendlier — small hue moves change personality — [Kennedy, HSB](https://www.learnui.design/blog/the-hsb-color-system-practicioners-primer.html), [Kennedy, colour framework](https://www.learnui.design/blog/color-in-ui-design-a-practical-framework.html)
51. Define fixed shade ramps up front: 8–10 greys, 1–2 primaries with 5–10 shades each, and a few accents for states (red = destructive, yellow = warning, green = positive). Pick the 500 (works as a button background), then 900 (text) and 100 (tinted background), then fill in. Tune by eye — shades generated on the fly with lighten/darken drift and multiply — [Schoger & Wathan](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette)
52. Start greys at a dark grey, not pure black, and tint greys slightly toward the brand hue (more saturation in the darker greys) — fully neutral greys look dead — [Schoger & Wathan](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette), [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
53. Tint borders, shadows and secondary text toward the background hue — pure black shadows and grey borders look pasted on — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
54. Use roughly 60% dominant colour, 30% secondary and 10% accent, with the accent the most vivid colour and reserved for the main action — the eye goes to the rarest, brightest colour — [Malewicz, 60-30-10](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/60-30-10-rule-in-ui)
55. Tone down a brand colour that overpowers the page by lowering its saturation, not its hue — it stays on brand but stops shouting — [Kennedy, HSB](https://www.learnui.design/blog/the-hsb-color-system-practicioners-primer.html)
56. Avoid oversaturated colours in dark mode — they vibrate and strain the eye — [Malewicz, 60-30-10](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/60-30-10-rule-in-ui)
57. Never use colour alone for status; add a text label — colour-blind users miss it — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines), [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)

### 2.6 Depth, light and detail
58. Light comes from above: raised elements (buttons, cards, dropdowns) get a lighter top and a darker bottom or shadow, and inset elements (inputs, tracks, unchecked boxes) get the opposite — depth tells people what they can press — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
59. Prefer "flat-ish" over purely flat: keep subtle shadows — removing every depth cue hobbles both style and meaning — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
60. Build shadows from at least two layers (ambient plus direct), and pair them with a semi-transparent border — one-layer shadows look like a default — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
61. Give shadows and other effects a meaning (depth, interactivity), not decoration — decoration without meaning reads as noise — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)
62. Find one motif (a shape, symbol or gradient) and repeat it across the logo, type, empty-space fillers, dividers, bullets, quotation marks and numbers. "Add more of the motif, not more decoration" — this is the main way a site feels like one brand instead of a template — [Kennedy, motif](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs-create-cohesive-brand.html)
63. On top of a solid base, add only a few accents from the toolbox: angled or curved section edges, a tilted shape behind an element, custom brand bullets, inline icons in a sentence, content breaking out of its frame, images that continue behind the next section, a faint dot grid, solid or offset shadows, oversized low-contrast background words — plain defaults look generic, but piling effects on looks worse — [Kennedy, 37 ways](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs.html)
64. Keep textures (patterns, lines, dot grids) faint, and choose only effects that match the brand's motif — arbitrary effects feel bolted on — [Kennedy, 37 ways](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs.html)
65. Let a small detail carry meaning (e.g. drop caps cut to echo the story's subject) — specific details make a page belong to its content — [Santa Maria, Interludes](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/interludes-in-design)
66. Ask which small conveniences users would miss if they vanished, and invest there — details build loyalty more than features do — [Boag, details](https://boagworld.com/emails/the-devil-is-in-the-details-and-the-details-sell/)

### 2.7 Imagery
67. Text over a photo needs help: a 35%-black overlay, a translucent box, a blurred and darkened area, a floor fade (0% black at the middle to about 20% at the bottom) or an elliptical scrim. Raw text straight on a photo is only safe with dark, low-detail images, and must be tested at every size — legibility breaks when the image or viewport changes — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
68. Do not rely on the natural blur in one particular photo for text legibility — swap the image and the text breaks — [Kennedy, 7 Rules pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
69. Set explicit image dimensions, preload only the images above the fold, lazy-load the rest, and prefer muted looping video over GIFs — layout shift and slow heroes feel cheap — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
70. Let images scale with their containers, but cap them so a wide screen does not push the text away — [Marcotte](https://alistapart.com/article/responsive-web-design/)

### 2.8 Motion
72. Most UI motion stays under 300ms: micro-interactions 100–150ms, standard 150–250ms, modals and drawers 200–300ms. Exits run about 20% faster than entrances, and bigger or farther movement takes longer. A 180ms dropdown feels faster than a 400ms one — slow motion on frequent actions reads as lag — [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste), [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
73. Use ease-out for things entering or leaving, ease-in-out for things moving on screen, ease for hover, and linear only for constant motion. Prefer custom curves to the CSS built-ins — ease-out's fast start feels responsive — [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste), [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
74. Press feedback: scale(0.97) on :active. Entrances start from scale 0.9–0.95, never 0 — things should not appear from nowhere — [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
75. Set transform-origin to the trigger (a popover grows from its button) — spatial origin explains cause and effect — [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips), [Freiberg, interaction design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)
76. Animate only transform and opacity, never transition: all, and offer a prefers-reduced-motion variant — layout properties drop frames, and some users get sick from motion — [Kowalski, great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations), [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
77. Do not animate what people use many times a day (menus, command palettes, hover on dense lists) or anything triggered from the keyboard. Save delight for rare moments — repeated delight becomes friction, and removing it makes tools feel faster — [Kowalski, you don't need animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations), [Freiberg, interaction design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)
78. Every animation must be interruptible, and user input wins over motion — locked transitions feel broken — [Freiberg, interaction design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design), [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
79. Delay the first tooltip; once one is open, show its neighbours instantly with no animation — this prevents accidental popups without slowing exploration — [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
80. When easing and timing cannot hide a rough crossfade, add about 2px of blur during it — the blur masks the gap between states — [Kowalski, 7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)
81. Marketing pages may animate more slowly than app UI — speed rules matter most for repeated actions — [Kowalski, you don't need animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations)
82. No autoplay except muted, non-essential loops, and anything longer than 5 seconds needs a pause control — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
83. Spinners and skeletons get a show delay (about 150–300ms) and a minimum visible time (about 300–500ms), and skeletons must match the final layout — this avoids flashes and jumps — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)

### 2.9 Interaction, forms and states
84. Tap targets are at least 44px on mobile (24px on desktop), and the hit area matches the visual — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines), [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
85. Anything that looks clickable must be clickable. Links are real `<a>` elements, so new-tab and right-click work — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
86. Keep the submit button enabled until submission starts, then show a spinner while keeping the label. Put errors beside their fields and focus the first one. Never block typing — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
87. Make destructive actions confirmable or undoable. Explain failures in plain words and always give a next step — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E), [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
88. Design empty, sparse, dense and error states, not just the ideal one — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
89. Make primary actions reachable one-handed on mobile — people use small-business sites on the go — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)
90. Behaviour must match the visual polish. Users primed by a slick page expect it to work as well as it looks — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)
91. Corners and screen edges are effectively infinite targets, so put frequent controls near them — Fitts's law — [Freiberg, interaction design](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)
92. Feel fast: render the first screen quickly, respond within milliseconds, and show progress when something is slow — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)

### 2.10 Copy and wording
93. Write button labels that say exactly what happens ("Save API Key", not "Continue"). Frame errors with how to fix them — [Web Interface Guidelines (Vercel-specific section)](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
94. Fold context into values instead of using labels ("12 left in stock") — shorter, warmer copy and a clearer hierarchy — [Schoger & Wathan](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/labels-are-a-last-resort)
95. Prefer inline help, with tooltips as a last resort — hidden help is rarely found — [Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
96. Use text as interface where it fits — Chimero lists it among the web-native patterns — [Chimero, Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)

### 2.11 Brand character and quality
97. Character comes from how ordinary things are done, not from complexity. A simple move done in your own way beats an elaborate borrowed one — [van Schneider, Style](https://vanschneider.com/blog/edition-258/)
98. Treat quality as a deliberate choice that costs effort: judge work by how it feels, performs and ages, not by how fast it was made — [van Schneider, Quality](https://vanschneider.com/blog/what-is-quality-anymore/)
99. Remove features or sections that no longer serve the vision, even if they were requested — restraint signals intent — [Lovin, quality](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)
100. Aim for clarity, not just simplicity: frame and explain complex offers rather than stripping them bare — [Chimero, Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)
101. Lead with a clear point of view that comes across within a quick scroll, rather than trying to be complete — [Zhuo / Soleio](https://lg.substack.com/p/how-to-spot-a-world-class-designer)
102. Make the experience coherent across every surface (pages, emails, print) — world-building is what separates great designers from good ones — [Zhuo / Soleio](https://lg.substack.com/p/how-to-spot-a-world-class-designer)

## 3. Their warnings about generic, templated or AI-looking design

- **Unstyled defaults** (small text, no line spacing, content to the edge) are the baseline look to escape. Starting from them produces cramped pages. [Kennedy pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
- **Too many colours in too many places** destroys a clean look. [Kennedy pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
- **Purely flat design** with no light or depth is weaker than it looks. [Kennedy pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
- **Default-looking parts** (stock bullets, boxy horizontal splits, plain layouts) read as boring. Swap a few for brand-specific versions, but don't stack effects. [Kennedy, 37 ways](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs.html)
- **Decoration without a motif** feels arbitrary. Repeat one idea everywhere instead. [Kennedy, motif](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs-create-cohesive-brand.html)
- **Overused fonts** (Montserrat is named) and **thoughtless defaults** like Helvetica-by-elimination are warnings. [Kennedy pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html), [Santa Maria](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/the-sweatpants-of-typefaces)
- **The web's recurring patterns** are flat colour with simple gradients, big horizontal bands, a blurred photo with large overlaid type, device mockups as props, and mosaic grids. Chimero calls them reasonable responses to the medium, but they should be chosen for the content, not by habit. **"Bicycle bear" sites**, flashy and over-engineered against the medium, impress for a moment and then fail. [Chimero, Web's Grain](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)
- **Copying the sector**: "no one in our sector does this" and "everyone uses this structure" are red flags. Copying competitors produces mediocre results at best. [Boag](https://boagworld.com/emails/copy-competitors/)
- **Derivative portfolios**: work that looks like one known designer silently costs opportunities. [Hische](https://www.jessicahische.is/thinkingthoughtsoninspiration)
- **Fast generation**: when passable design can be generated in minutes, teams must decide what is worth building and which values the product expresses. Without principles, work looks random, vague and dull. [Friedman](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/04/practical-guide-design-principles/)
- **Agents without rules** guess or invent motion and type standards. The remedy is strict written rules with exact values. [Kowalski, agents](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/agents-with-taste)
- **Convenience culture** dulls the ability to recognise quality, and disposable, fast-made output is the norm to resist. [van Schneider, Quality](https://vanschneider.com/blog/what-is-quality-anymore/)
- **Gratuitous motion**: delight that repeats daily turns into friction, and animations without a purpose should be cut. [Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations), [Freiberg](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)

## 4. Disagreements

1. **Grids and type scales.**
   - Kennedy says beginners should skip column grids and ratio-based type scales: align to content and hand-tune sizes. [Kennedy, beginners](https://www.learnui.design/blog/why-beginning-designers-dont-need-grids-type-scales-color-theory.html)
   - Malewicz prescribes a precise 12-column, 64/32px grid. [Malewicz](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/my-secret-to-perfect-layout-revealed)
   - The Web Interface Guidelines require aligning to a grid or baseline. [WIG](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)
   - Design-system advice usually sides with tokens and scales.
   - Kennedy still keeps consistent sizes across pages, so the real split is whether the numbers are derived from a ratio or tuned by eye.
2. **Colour structure.**
   - Kennedy builds everything from one base hue and rejects colour-wheel harmonies. [Kennedy](https://www.learnui.design/blog/color-in-ui-design-a-practical-framework.html)
   - Malewicz teaches a 60-30-10 split across distinct hues. [Malewicz](https://hype4.academy/articles/design/60-30-10-rule-in-ui)
   - Schoger and Wathan plan fixed multi-hue ramps (primary, accents and greys) up front. They agree with Kennedy on "no on-the-fly lighten/darken", but go beyond a single hue. [Refactoring UI](https://www.refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette)
3. **Depth versus flat.**
   - Kennedy argues against pure flat design and for top-lit shadows. [Kennedy pt 1](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-1.html)
   - Chimero calls flat colour and simple gradients a reasonable, web-native response rather than a failure. [Chimero](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/)
4. **Decoration.**
   - Kennedy's "37 ways" encourages tilted shapes, patterned shadows and hand-drawn borders.
   - Chimero warns against flashy "bicycle bear" sites.
   - Lovin says visual treatments should carry meaning, not decorate.
   - They meet on "a few, on-motif, on a solid base". [Kennedy](https://www.learnui.design/blog/spice-up-designs.html), [Chimero](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2015/the-webs-grain/), [Lovin](https://brianlovin.com/writing/quality-software-YA7uK4E)
5. **Motion amount.**
   - Kowalski and Freiberg cut motion from frequent actions and keep it under 300ms. [Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/you-dont-need-animations)
   - Kowalski himself allows slower, richer motion on marketing pages, which is where small-business sites sit.
   - Common component-library defaults animate everything.
6. **Taste versus principles.**
   - Friedman says taste should not drive decisions; goals and written principles should. [Friedman](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2026/04/practical-guide-design-principles/)
   - Kowalski, van Schneider and Hische treat trained taste and personal style as the core skill. [Kowalski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/developing-taste), [van Schneider](https://vanschneider.com/blog/edition-258/)
   - Zhuo and Soleio favour one owner deciding over consensus.
7. **Copying.**
   - Kennedy says "steal like an artist": imitate the best (Dribbble, Mobbin) until you are good. [Kennedy pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
   - Hische says copy only in private and never publish derivative work. [Hische](https://www.jessicahische.is/thinkingthoughtsoninspiration)
   - Boag says do not copy competitors at all and look outside the sector. [Boag](https://boagworld.com/emails/copy-competitors/)
   - For a small-business site, the safe combination is to study widely and privately, then build from the client's own content and motif.
8. **Fonts.**
   - Kennedy steers beginners toward free neutral sans fonts. [Kennedy pt 2](https://www.learnui.design/blog/7-rules-for-creating-gorgeous-ui-part-2.html)
   - Santa Maria warns that neutral defaults chosen by elimination are the problem, and that the font should fit the job. [Santa Maria](https://v5.jasonsantamaria.com/articles/the-sweatpants-of-typefaces)
9. **Tooling and complexity.**
   - Chimero argues against build-heavy stacks for small sites and for working with plain HTML and CSS. [Chimero](https://frankchimero.com/blog/2018/everything-easy/)
   - The usual design-system advice, components plus tokens plus pipelines, assumes scale that small sites lack.
